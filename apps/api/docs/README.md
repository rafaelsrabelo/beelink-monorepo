The port gained `subscriptions` (by reference), `createSubscription` (always `billingType: UNDEFINED`) and `removeSubscription`; `billing-refusals.ts` holds the two refusals both services answer with. The page's status is not moved by the choice. **A page's payment (BEELINK-221):** `subscription-sync.service.ts` (`SubscriptionSync`) is the one door a fact from Asaas about a page's subscription comes in by, whoever made bee-link look — the webhook, the routine, the panel's `POST /stores/:slug/subscription/check`, or a choice that came upon a payment (which lets its claim go first): the subscription the row names and its charges read at Asaas with no lock held, then written under the row's lock, and only while the row still names that subscription, no choice holds its claim, and no read begun later was written (`asaasCheckedAt`). `subscription-facts.ts` says what Asaas's word comes to — the status, `currentPeriodEndsAt` (the midnight in Brasília closing the next due day, by the calendar counted from `firstDueDate`: a late payment gains no days), the way it was paid and of a card its brand and last four digits, the invoice waiting whose period is not over, and `nextCheckAt` — and `subscription-status.ts` now also reads an `ACTIVE` page past `currentPeriodEndsAt` as `PAST_DUE`. `billing-webhook.controller.ts` takes bee-link's own Asaas webhook, refusing a request without `ASAAS_PLATFORM_WEBHOOK_TOKEN` (compared in constant time in `platform-webhook.config.ts`, the one file that names it) before anything is written; `billing-events.service.ts` (`BillingEvents`) writes each event once to `billing_events`, answers, and works it through the door with retries — its body never applied. `platform-webhook-keeper.ts` registers that webhook at the account and sets it going again; `billing-routine.ts` runs, every minute, the events due, the pages whose `nextCheckAt` came, the webhook once a day and the pruning — never in a test, with charging off or with no key. **A page locked for want of payment (BEELINK-223):** `page-lock.ts` is the one rule (`lockOf`): never an exempt page, nor one inside a free month (`freePeriodEndsAt`); `TRIAL_EXPIRED` from the instant a trial ends unpaid; a page past due open through its grace — seven days after `currentPeriodEndsAt`, or after the day it was found past due when there is no period (its only payment contested), written on the row as `graceEndsAt` and never moved earlier — and `SUSPENDED` after it; a cancelled one open while its paid period runs, `CANCELED` after; and `lockChangesAt`, when what it answers may stop holding by the clock alone. `subscription-status.ts` reads a page past due whose grace ended as `SUSPENDED`, and the door (`subscription-facts.ts`) writes it so, with `graceEndsAt`. `page-locks.service.ts` (`PageLocks`) says whether this deployment locks at all — `BILLING_ENFORCED` on **and** the key, so a page always has somewhere to pay — how one page stands, and, as the routine's clock (`settle`), writes what the clock alone moved: a trial expired, a period run out (past due with its grace, or suspended), a grace written, a page suspended — each one `UPDATE … WHERE` whose condition is what makes it due, so a payment written meanwhile is never undone. `page-lock.guard.ts` (`PageLockGuard`, registered in `AppModule` right after `JwtAuthGuard`) closes every route with a page in its path (`:storeSlug`, or the stores' own `:slug`) to a locked page: `403 PAGE_LOCKED` with `details.reason` to its owner alone — a stranger, a slug nobody holds and no session are answered as they are today — and `403 STORE_UNAVAILABLE`, never why, to a shop window's visitor and customer. A route says otherwise with `page-lock.decorators.ts`: `@OpenWhileLocked()` — the page's own subscription, and what a customer already has (the account and Google's door, addresses, data and its erasure, cashback, orders read and cancelled, an order's payment and conversation, reviews, the real-time ticket) — and `@AnswersLockedPage()`, the shop's public read, which the guard hands how the page stands (`@PageLockOf()`). Where nothing locks the guard returns before anything is read. **A coupon of bee-link's (BEELINK-224):** `platform-coupons.service.ts` (`PlatformCoupons`) gives a page that never paid one free month, once in its life, behind `POST /stores/:slug/subscription/coupon` — every refusal said before anything is written, then, in one transaction holding the page's row, the coupon's count moved by an `UPDATE` that passes only while the coupon holds (a `CHECK` holds the limit too), the use written (`platform_coupon_redemptions`, unique by the page's subscription) and `freePeriodEndsAt` set; a page whose row names a subscription at Asaas has it taken out first by `StoreSubscriptions.withdraw`, the cycle's own talk, and any other is given its month with Asaas never asked. `platform-coupon-rules.ts` holds what that reads without asking: the code as kept (trimmed, upper case, the shape a shop's coupon has), why a coupon gives nothing (switched off, past its last day, used up), which pages may use one (never paid: `TRIALING`, `TRIAL_EXPIRED`), and the month — from the latest of now, the trial's end and a free period still running, to `freeMonthEndOf` (`billing-dates.ts`), the midnight in Brasília closing the same day a month later, which BEELINK-297 calls with 13/10/2026. `platform-coupon.command.ts` is the command that makes, lists and switches coupons off, run in the api container through `src/scripts/platform-coupon.ts`; nothing else writes the two tables (gate `api/platform-coupons-in-billing`). **The pages that existed on 13/10/2026 (BEELINK-297):** the migration `20261015170000_pages_that_existed` gave each that never paid its free month, to the end of 13/11/2026 in Brasília (`PAGES_EXISTED_ON` in `billing.constants.ts`, `freeMonthEndOf`), and every page with no subscription the row a new page is born with — never shortening a later month, never touching an exempt page or one that paid. Switching charging on is prepared from a terminal: `billing-command.ts` is what the commands share (options, refusals, exit codes, `runInTerminal` for `src/scripts/`); `billing-report.ts` sorts the pages by how they stand at an instant, asking `lockOf` and `statusAt` and nothing else, and `billing-report.command.ts` prints it; `page-exemption.command.ts` makes a page that never paid exempt and back, by an `UPDATE` that a choice of a cycle in course leaves alone; `charging-notice.command.ts` writes the notice that charging starts, one a shopkeeper ever (`charging_notices`, unique by account), `charging-notice.ts` says the day each page locks — `lockStartOf` (`page-lock.ts`), the lock's own answers walked from the day charging starts — and `charging-notice-mailer.ts` (`ChargingNoticeMailer`) is the outbox the running API sends them from
│   ├── panel-counts/           # the numbers beside the panel's menu items (BEELINK-309), in one answer: who owns the shop is settled once, and each area lends its counting function — `countOpenOrders` (`orders/open-orders.ts`), `shopUnreadOf` (`conversations/shop-unread.ts`), `unseenReviewsOf` (`reviews/reviews-unseen.ts`) — the same ones those areas' own endpoints run. It imports no area's module

## Endpoints

Every route needs `Authorization: Bearer <access token>` unless it is marked public. Errors always answer `{ statusCode, errorCode, message }`; clients switch on `errorCode`.

| Verb | Path | Public | What | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `GET` | `/api/health` | yes | liveness — does not touch the database | `200` |
| `POST` | `/api/auth/register` | yes | create an account, e-mail the verification link | `201` · `409 AUTH_EMAIL_TAKEN` |
| `POST` | `/api/auth/verify-email` | yes | verify with the link's token | `204` · `400 AUTH_TOKEN_INVALID` |
| `POST` | `/api/auth/resend-verification` | yes | send the link again | `202`, for any address |
| `POST` | `/api/auth/login` | yes | open a session | `200 AuthSession` · `401 AUTH_INVALID_CREDENTIALS` · `403 AUTH_EMAIL_NOT_VERIFIED` |
| `POST` | `/api/auth/refresh` | yes | rotate the refresh token | `200 AuthSession` · `401 AUTH_TOKEN_INVALID` · `401 AUTH_REFRESH_REUSED` |
| `POST` | `/api/auth/logout` | yes | end this device's session | `204`, idempotent |
| `POST` | `/api/auth/forgot-password` | yes | e-mail a reset link | `202`, for any address |
| `POST` | `/api/auth/reset-password` | yes | set a new password, end every session | `204` · `400 AUTH_TOKEN_INVALID` |
| `GET` | `/api/users/me` | no | the signed-in person | `200 User` · `401 AUTH_UNAUTHENTICATED` |
| `POST` | `/api/stores` | no | open a shop — claims the slug, under the address's lock (BEELINK-331): an address another page left is taken too. `template` (`OpeningTemplateId`) is the model its home opens with, published with the store: a site's, or one of a shop's four home models, arranged as on a shop with nothing on its shelf (`store-opening.ts`, BEELINK-266). Absent — or not for the store's type — a shop opens with its default page and a site with `servicos-b2b` | `201 Store` · `400 STORE_SLUG_RESERVED` · `409 STORE_SLUG_TAKEN` · `404 STORE_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND` |
| `GET` | `/api/stores/mine` | no | the signed-in shopkeeper's shops, newest first | `200 Store[]` |
| `GET` | `/api/stores/:slug/public` | yes | the shop window — the narrow storefront shape, with `metaPixelId`, the shop's Meta Pixel ID or null (BEELINK-269), and `googleAnalyticsId`, its GA4 measurement ID or null (BEELINK-301) | `200 PublicStore` · `404 STORE_NOT_FOUND` |
| `GET` | `/api/stores/:slug` | no | the shop as its owner edits it | `200 Store` · `403 STORE_FORBIDDEN` · `404 STORE_NOT_FOUND` |
| `PUT` | `/api/stores/:slug` | no | replace what the panel edits — a full body, not a patch | `200 Store` · `400` on `slug`/`latitude`/`longitude` · `403 STORE_FORBIDDEN` · `404 STORE_NOT_FOUND` |

Nothing else reads the table, and it has no status and no link to `users`. Its own per-IP limit, `COURIER_APPLICATION_RATE_LIMIT_*`. The body is never logged, and a write the database refuses is logged by its code alone: Postgres's detail carries the row | `204` · `400 COURIER_NAME_INVALID` · `COURIER_DOCUMENT_INVALID` · `COURIER_EMAIL_INVALID` · `COURIER_WHATSAPP_INVALID` · `COURIER_CITY_NOT_OPEN` · `COURIER_VEHICLE_INVALID` · `COURIER_TERMS_REQUIRED` (the first field refused, in that order; a field left out is refused like a wrong one) · `400 BAD_REQUEST` for a field the route does not know · `409 COURIER_DOCUMENT_TAKEN` · `COURIER_EMAIL_TAKEN` · `COURIER_WHATSAPP_TAKEN` · `429 RATE_LIMITED` |

`GET /api/stores/mine` is declared above `GET /api/stores/:slug`: Nest matches in declaration order, and `mine` is on the reserved-slug list so no shop can occupy it either.

Ownership is explicit code, not a database policy: `StoresService.assertOwnership()` is the single gate every owner-facing read and every write passes through. A shop owned by somebody else answers **403**, not 404 — `/<slug>` is a public storefront, so its existence leaks nothing.

Rate limited per IP, all answering `429 RATE_LIMITED`: register, login, forgot-password and resend-verification; `POST /api/stores` and `PUT /api/stores/:slug`, because each can drive an outbound geocoding call this API waits up to four seconds on; and `GET /api/stores/:slug/public`, generously — every browser reaches it through the web app's server, so the limit sees one address for the whole storefront. `POST /api/courier-applications` has a limit of its own, sized for a person who mistypes and tries again. The key is the address only because `TRUST_PROXY` lets the web app forward it: the plugin runs in Fastify's `onRequest`, before `JwtAuthGuard` has decoded the token, so there is no owner to key on.

Nothing caps how many shops one owner may open. The rate limit bounds the rate, not the total.

`store_categories` and the six colour presets are both platform data, and they are kept differently on purpose. The taxonomy is a table, because a shop points at a row of it, and it is seeded by [`prisma/seed/store-categories.sql`](../prisma/seed/store-categories.sql) — `pnpm --filter api db:seed`, or automatically by `prisma migrate dev`. The presets are a constant in the module, because nothing references a preset: applying one writes its four colours onto the shop, and the shop keeps the colours, not the choice. They are served rather than shipped as source because `web/no-hex-colors` scans `apps/web/src` and `packages/ui/src`, and twenty-four colour literals in either tree are the exact thing that gate exists to stop.

Swagger documents all of it at `/api/docs`, with an Authorize button that holds a bearer token. `@ApiBearerAuth()` goes on a handler, never on a controller class that also holds a `@Public()` route — on the class it marked the anonymous storefront read as needing a token.

## bee-link's coupons, from a terminal

No screen makes a coupon yet: the backoffice is an app of its own, still to come. A coupon of bee-link's (BEELINK-224) is made, listed and switched off by a command run where the API runs — from `apps/api` after `pnpm --filter api build`, and in production in the `api` container, as [deploy.md](../../../docs/repo/deploy.md#bee-links-own-plans) says.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `node dist/scripts/platform-coupon.js create --code <CODE> [--expires <YYYY-MM-DD>] [--max-uses <N>]` | makes one: the code kept in upper case — 3 to 30 of A–Z, 0–9, `-` and `_`, beginning with a letter or a digit — its last day on Brasília's calendar (today or later; left out, it never expires) and how many pages may use it (1 to 1,000,000; left out, no limit). A code taken, whatever its case, is refused |
| `node dist/scripts/platform-coupon.js list` | every coupon, the newest first: its code, where it stands (`active`, `disabled`, `expired`, `used up`), its last day, the pages that used it and its limit, the day it was made |
| `node dist/scripts/platform-coupon.js disable --code <CODE>` | no page uses it from then on; the months it gave stay given. There is no `enable`: a code switched off by mistake is a new code |

It writes to the terminal and never to a log, and exits `0`, `1` when refused (a code taken, a day gone by, a coupon nobody made) or `2` when asked wrongly, with how it is used. **No real code is ever written in this repository, which is public** — not in a seed, not in a test (theirs all say `TESTE`), not in a document.

## Switching charging on, from a terminal

Three more commands (BEELINK-297) prepare the day charging is switched on, run as the coupon's is; [deploy.md](../../../docs/repo/deploy.md#switching-charging-on) says in what order. Each writes to the terminal alone and exits `0`, `1` when refused — nothing written — or `2` when asked wrongly.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `node dist/scripts/billing-report.js [--on <YYYY-MM-DD>]` | the pages as charging would find them now, or as that day begins in Brasília (today or later): how many, exempt, with no subscription, locked with charging on and by which reason, open on a plan paid for, open by a free period by its last day, open in their trial; and apart the pages created after 13/10/2026 whose trial is over — they lock the day charging is switched on — by address, day created and trial's last day. Each page counts once, as `lockOf` and `statusAt` answer at that instant. It says whether this deployment locks pages (`BILLING_ENFORCED` and the key) |
| `node dist/scripts/page-exemption.js exempt --slug <SLUG>` | makes a page that never paid exempt: never locked, never charged. Its trial and free month stay on its row. Refused for a page that paid (its subscription at Asaas would go on charging), while its owner is choosing a plan, and for an address no page has. One that chose a plan and never paid is exempted, and told that the subscription stands at Asaas, where it charges nobody and may be removed |
| `node dist/scripts/page-exemption.js unexempt --slug <SLUG>` | back to a page that never paid, its trial and free month as they were; refused for one made exempt by hand after it paid |
| `node dist/scripts/page-exemption.js list` | the exempt pages: address, day created, name |
| `node dist/scripts/charging-notice.js send --on <YYYY-MM-DD>` | writes the e-mail that tells each shopkeeper with a page that never paid and is not exempt the day charging starts — seven days from today or later — the price of each cycle by page, read from the plan on sale, and the day each of their pages locks without a plan. One per account, ever: a second run writes only for an account not told yet, and one with another day is refused. The running API sends them from its outbox within a minute, read when they go; a notice whose pages no longer lock is done without an e-mail |
| `node dist/scripts/charging-notice.js status` | how the notices stand — done, waiting, given up — and the address of each given up, to be told some other way |
