# Product — bee-link

> **Tier:** product — true no matter which app is rewritten. See [the tier map](../README.md).

**bee-link** lets a small shopkeeper sell without building a shop. They sign up, describe their store, load their catalogue, and immediately have two things: a public shop window their customers can open from a link, and a panel where orders arrive. There is no marketplace and no shared storefront — each store is its own destination, and bee-link never stands between a shopkeeper and their customer.

## Language

Every surface speaks the reader's language, between Brazilian Portuguese (`pt-BR`, the default) and English. A person's choice is remembered; without one, the browser's `Accept-Language` decides. Screens, e-mails and the sentences a person reads follow it.

The shopkeeper's customers are Brazilian, so the shop window and the order messages are written in pt-BR. English exists for the owner-facing screens.

What the API sends is a stable `errorCode`, never a sentence — the apps own the words, so adding a language never touches the backend. Identifiers and comments stay in English everywhere, whatever the screen says.

## Accounts

An **account** is how a person proves who they are. A shopkeeper has one, and it is **bee-link's**: it opens the panel. A store's affiliate signs in with one of bee-link's too, which opens their own area (see Affiliates). A customer who chose to be remembered has one too, and it is **the shop's**: opened at one shop, it exists there and nowhere else — not at another shop, not in the panel. The same person buying from two shops has two accounts, with two passwords, and signing in at one is not signing in at the other.

- It has a **name**, an **e-mail** and a **password** (an account opened through Google has none until it sets one). No two accounts of bee-link share an e-mail, and no two accounts of one shop do, compared without case and without surrounding spaces; the same e-mail may have an account at every shop and one more at bee-link.
- A password has between 8 and 128 characters.
- An account is born **unverified**. Signing up sends an e-mail with a confirmation link, valid for 24 hours and usable once. Asking again sends a new link.
- An unverified account cannot sign in with a password.
- Signing in opens a **session** on that device, at the door the account belongs to: the panel, or its shop. A session lives while it is used at least once every 30 days; signing out ends that device's session only.
- A forgotten password is replaced through an e-mailed link, valid for 1 hour and usable once. Replacing the password ends every session of the account.
- Whether an e-mail has an account is never revealed: "forgot password" and "resend confirmation" answer the same way for any address, and a wrong password reads the same as an unknown e-mail. The checkout says nothing of it either: buying as a visitor answers the same for every e-mail (see Customers).
- Every account is opened under **bee-link's terms of use**, having read its **privacy policy**, both public at the site's root. Opening one says so beside the button, whether it is a form or "Continuar com Google", and the account keeps a record of the version it accepted and when. Continuing with Google, or setting a password from an e-mailed link, records it too when the account has no record of the version in force, or when it is what proves the e-mail belongs to whoever holds the account. The texts carry one version, the day they took effect, and a new version is a new record, never an edit of the old one. The privacy policy says who answers for which data: the shopkeeper for their shop's customers, with bee-link as the one processing it for them, and bee-link for the shopkeepers' own accounts.
- A customer may **take a copy** of everything their shop keeps about them — the account, the record, the addresses, the orders, the favourites, the reviews and the conversations — as one file, whenever they like.
- A customer may **delete their account**, confirming it with the password, or with the e-mail typed again when the account was opened through Google and has none. Every session ends at once, and with the account goes everything the customer kept for themselves: favourites, saved addresses, CPF, birth date, notice preferences and the record of the terms accepted. The shop keeps its books: a customer it sold to stays on its list with a name and a phone, each order keeps the name and address it recorded, its conversation stays readable to the shop, and their reviews stay, signed "Cliente". A customer the shop never sold to leaves with the account. The same e-mail may open a new account there, which starts with nothing of the old one.

## Stores

A **store** is the tenant. Everything else in the product — products, orders, customers, coupons, delivery rules — belongs to exactly one store and is meaningless outside it.

- A store has an **owner**, which is one account. An account may own more than one store.
- A store has a **slug**, unique across the product, and the slug is its public address: the shop window is `/<slug>` and the panel is `/admin/<slug>`. A slug is what a shopkeeper prints on a flyer, so it changes rarely and never silently.
- Its owner may **change the slug** — a page created with the wrong name is the case it exists for — at most once every 30 days, after a question that says what changing does. The slug it leaves is never anyone else's: it stays the store's, reserved for good, and leads to the new one — the shop window, a product, the cart, the panel, with the rest of the address kept — so a flyer already printed and an e-mail already sent go on working. The store may take a slug it left back. What lived on the old address in a visitor's browser does not follow: a customer signed in there signs in again, and a cart in progress starts empty. A domain of the store's own does not change.
- A store may have a **domain of its own** — `minhaloja.com.br` — in place of the slug's address. Its owner points the domain at bee-link, and once that is checked the domain is the shop's one address: the shop window opens at its root, no address of the shop carries the slug, and the slug's address leads there for good, so a link already shared goes on working. A domain belongs to one store and a store has at most one. The panel stays at `/admin/<slug>`.
- A store has a **kind**, which says how it sells rather than what it sells, and changes what the shop window emphasises, not what the product can do. One kind exists: a shop that sells online end to end. A second one is earned by a shop window that has to behave differently — never by a new line of business.
- A store has a **category**, chosen from a list the product seeds. That is where a line of business lives — supplements, fashion, groceries — and it is directory data: it tells a visitor what the shop is, and changes nothing about how the shop works.
- A store carries its own **brand**: a name, a logo, and colours that the shop window wears. The colours are data the shopkeeper chooses, not a theme the product ships. The brand reaches the browser's tab too: a store may choose an **icon** for it, a small square picture; a store that chose none shows its logo there, and one with neither shows bee-link's. The panel is bee-link's and always shows bee-link's. And it reaches a link someone shares: the preview of any page of a store says the store's name and shows its logo on the colour of the top of its shop window — or the page's own picture, where it has one: a product's photo, a landing's. A store with no logo shows its cover, and one with neither shows no picture.
- A store has an **address** and a position on the map. That position is what delivery distance is measured from.
- **Only the owner may read or change anything of theirs.** This is decided by the product, once, at the point the data is served — not by the screen that happens to be asking. Two shopkeepers are strangers to each other. An affiliate reads, of a store they sell for, their own standing with it and nothing else (see Affiliates).

## What a page costs

bee-link is paid for **by the page**. A page is a store, and the plan, the trial and the standing below are the page's, not the account's that owns it.

- **Each page has its own.** An account with two pages has two trials and two subscriptions, and pays for two. A page that stops for want of payment stops alone: the same account's other pages go on.
- **A new page is tried for 7 days, with no card asked.** The trial is counted from the day the page was created and ends with a day, not with an hour: at midnight in Brasília, closing the seventh day after that one. So a page is never tried for less than seven whole days.
- **A plan is paid by the cycle**: by the month, for six months or for a year, each charged once for the whole of it, the longer ones costing less a month. A plan need not be sold in every cycle. **Beelink** is the plan on sale; **Beelink + Beelia** is registered and not sold until Beelia exists.
- **A renewal falls on the first charge's day of the month**, and on the month's last day where the month lacks it: a subscription first charged on the 31st of October renews on the 30th of November, and then on the 31st of December.
- **A page stands in one of seven ways**: *in trial*; *trial expired* — the trial ended and the page never paid; *active* — a paid period is running; *past due* — it paid before, and its period ended with the next charge unpaid; *suspended* — stopped for want of payment, having paid before; *cancelled* — having paid, its subscription ended, removed or with the period's money given back, and what was paid is used until its end; *exempt* — it owes nothing, by bee-link's own choice, whatever is paid or not.
- **A payment is what moves a page, and bee-link asks Asaas whether there was one.** A page is active from the moment Asaas holds the payment of its period — bee-link hears of it from Asaas, looks again on its own in case the news was lost, and asks at once when the panel wants to know. A paid period runs until the end of the next due day, by Asaas's calendar: paying late gains no days, and a renewal paid before its day adds its period to the one running. A subscription chosen and never paid leaves the page as it was — in trial, or with its trial expired — even once it is removed. Money given back takes the paid period with it; a subscription removed after paying leaves it to run.
- **It is bee-link charging the shopkeeper, apart from the shop's sales.** A customer's payment goes to the shop's own Asaas account and never meets what the page pays bee-link.
- **Who is charged is the account, once for all its pages.** To be charged, a shopkeeper gives a name — their own or a company's — and a CPF or a CNPJ; the e-mail is the account's own, and neither a phone nor an address is asked. Each page still has its own subscription.
- **A plan is bought by choosing its cycle, and paid at Asaas's own page.** The shopkeeper chooses for one page, and is handed the invoice of that page's first charge, due the same day: a card, Pix or a boleto, chosen there — no card is ever typed into bee-link. Choosing does not make the page active; the payment does. Until it is paid the choice can be made again: the same cycle is the same invoice, another cycle replaces it, and a page never has two subscriptions. What is charged is what the plan costs on the day it is chosen. A page that pays during its trial gives the trial's remaining days up: its paid period runs from its first charge's due day, the day the cycle was chosen. The panel says so before the invoice is opened, with what each cycle costs that day, comes to a month and saves against the month.
- **A coupon of bee-link's gives a page one free month — never a discount.** It is for a page that never paid, and a page uses one in its life, whatever the coupon. The month begins when the free time the page already has ends — its trial, or the month the pages that existed on 13/10/2026 were given — or at once when it has none, so no free day is given up; and it ends with the same day of the next month, closing at midnight in Brasília, as a paid month does. Nothing is charged for it and nothing is made at Asaas: an invoice the page had chosen and not paid is withdrawn, as choosing another cycle withdraws it. While it runs the page is open, whatever it stood at, and the panel says until when it is free; when it ends the page goes on as a page that never paid — locked until it pays. A coupon has a code, and may have a last day and a limit of pages, after which it gives nothing; bee-link makes coupons and switches them off, which takes back no month already given. The panel offers the code beside the cycles.
- **Of a card, bee-link keeps its brand and its last four digits**, and nothing else.
- **A page that does not pay is locked, and alone**: the account's other pages go on. A page whose trial ended unpaid is locked at once. One whose paid period ran out with the renewal unpaid works as ever for **seven days of grace** — counted from the end of the period, or, with no period to count from, from the day it was found behind — and is suspended and locked when they end; the date the grace ends is kept, so the lock and whatever warns of it say the same day. A cancelled page works until the end of what it paid for. A free month given by a coupon keeps any page open until it ends, and then the rule applies as for any page; an exempt page is never locked. Paying opens a locked page at once.
- **A locked page's panel shows nothing but its subscription**, which is where it is paid; the account, its other pages and creating a new one are untouched. **Its shop window is off the air**: the shop's name, that it is unavailable right now, and nothing it sells — no product, no price, no basket, no new order — and nobody is told why. It is not offered to search engines while it is locked. **A customer who already bought there keeps what they have**: they still sign in, read their orders, pay, cancel and talk about one already made, and see their own account and data. Around those pages the shop offers nothing to buy: no search, no basket, no menu of its products, no order by WhatsApp.
- **The owner of a locked page is told why on every screen of its panel, and how to pay**: the trial ended, the free month ended, the renewal went unpaid past its grace, or the subscription was cancelled — with the day it happened, what each period of the plan costs the page, and the way to the screen where it is paid. The menu, the switch to another page and the account stay up. Once paid, the panel comes back as it was, without signing out.
- **A page about to lock says so first**, over every screen of its panel: on the last two days of its trial and on its last day — or of its free month, which is looked at first, since a page in it does not lock when its trial ends — and through the grace of an unpaid renewal, with the last day to pay. Only where the page can lock: with charging on and bee-link able to charge.
- **The panel's menu says where the open page stands**, above its settings, and leads to where it is paid: in its trial and how many days are left, free until when, renewing when, its payment overdue, cancelled and used until when, or locked. It says so more strongly on the last two days of a trial or a free month, behind on a renewal, or locked — only where the page can lock. With charging off, or for an exempt page, the menu shows no plan.
- **Every page that existed on 13/10/2026 was given one free month, counted from that day**: it is free until 13/11/2026, whatever the day charging is switched on, and after that the rule applies as for any page. A page created since has its own trial alone. The month is not a coupon: such a page may still use one, whose month begins when this one ends.
- **bee-link chooses the pages it charges nothing for** — its own, test pages, a pilot shop: an *exempt* page is never locked and never charged. Only a page that never paid is made exempt, and it may be made an ordinary page again, its trial and free month as they were.
- **Every shopkeeper is told before charging starts**, by e-mail, at least seven days ahead and once: the day it starts, what each cycle costs a page, and the day each of their pages locks without a plan. A shopkeeper whose pages are all exempt or paid for is not told.
- **Affiliates cost nothing while they are tried.** bee-link means to charge a page for each active affiliate, by the month; until that is built, and every shopkeeper is told before it starts, no page is charged for one.
- **None of it is charged yet.** Until bee-link switches charging on, nothing is charged, no page is locked, and the panel shows no plan, no notice and no payment screen; a page's trial is counted all the same. Pages lock only where a page has somewhere to pay.

## The shop window

The shop window at `/<slug>` is **anonymous**. No one signs in to buy, nothing about a visitor is required before they choose, and the page must be readable by a search engine and by a phone on a bad connection. It is the surface the product is judged on.

It shows the store's brand, its catalogue grouped by category, and a product's detail with its images. A visitor builds a **cart**, which is theirs and stays on their device until they check out.

A showcase — a row of products on a page — offers **"ver tudo"** only when it leads somewhere with more than the row already shows: its category, for a category's showcase, and the whole catalogue for any other. A link over every product there is promises what is not there.

The **search** is the header's protagonist: the widest thing in it, right after the logo. While someone types it suggests products with their price, and a product sold one way only goes into the cart from there, with no page in between; one with options is chosen on its page.

Most visitors are on a phone, so a list of products — the catalogue, a category, a search — **grows as it is scrolled** rather than asking for a page number: the next products are on their way before the visitor reaches the end, and coming back from a product returns them to where they were in the list. Each page still has its own address, which is what a search engine follows and what a visitor whose phone has not run the page's script gets.

A shop window that is briefly out of date is acceptable; a shop window that is slow or unreadable is not.

## The panel

The panel at `/admin/<slug>` is where the shopkeeper works: the catalogue, the orders as they arrive, the store's own settings, delivery rules, promotions, and what sold. It is signed in, it is used on a phone behind a counter as often as on a desk, and a new order has to become visible without the shopkeeper reloading anything.

Two things in the panel's frame move by themselves, and they answer different questions. The **bell** is the general feed — news of any kind, as it arrives: today a new order, a payment approved, a customer's message. A **menu item's number** is specific to its area, and says how much is waiting there right now: "Pedidos" counts the orders **em aberto** — every order still asking something of the shop, which is every status but delivered and cancelled, whoever placed it — "Conversas" the conversations with a message nobody at the shop read, "Avaliações" the reviews written since the list was last opened. An item with nothing waiting shows no number, and the orders list's "Em aberto" filter is the same rule, so the menu's number is that list's total.

## Catalogue

- A **product** belongs to a store. It has a name, a description, a price and images.
- A photo is **not required**. A product with none is drawn everywhere a photo goes — the shop window, its page, the cart, an order, the panel — with **bee-link's own picture**, the same for every shop. That picture is never stored on the product: the first photo the shopkeeper adds takes its place everywhere at once.
- A shopkeeper may **import products from a document** — a PDF of the menu or catalogue, or a photo of it. An AI reads it, and nothing is created until the shopkeeper has reviewed what was read: they untick, rename, price and file each product, and only then import. What is imported goes on sale at once, with its categories and its flavours or sizes. A name the shop already has is refused, as it is when a product is written by hand.
- Whether a customer sees it is **two facts, not one**, and the product keeps them apart on purpose.
  - Its **status** is what the shopkeeper intends: *active*, or a *draft* nobody but them can see. A draft is something being written, not something that ran out.
  - Its **stock** is what the shelf says. A shop may choose not to count a product at all — most here sell made to order — and one that is not counted is never sold out. A shop that does count and reaches zero has a product that is **sold out**, which is a different thing from a draft and is fixed a different way.
- The shopkeeper may put **several products on sale, or back to draft, at once** from the list: they tick products — one by one or a whole page — across pages, and the choice is applied to all of them or to none.
- A product is in the shop window when it is active **and** not sold out. A draft is nowhere. A sold-out product leaves the window, the category and the search, but **keeps its own page**, marked sold out and with no way to order: that address is what a shopkeeper sends on WhatsApp, and a link that starts answering "not found" is the most visible failure this product can produce.
- A **category** also belongs to a store; a product sits in one. Both products and categories carry an **order the shopkeeper chooses** — the shop window shows them in that order, because the shopkeeper knows what they want to sell first. A new product goes last. The shopkeeper arranges the products among all of them at once, as a list or as the shop window draws them, and nothing changes for a visitor until the order is saved. One order holds for the whole shop: a category shows its products in it, and so does every showcase but the two with an order of their own, the newest first and the hand-picked. A visitor may still sort by price. A draft or a sold-out product keeps its place without being shown.
- A category may carry a **picture**. On the shop's front page the categories are drawn with the picture and the name under it or, where the shopkeeper says so, as **the picture alone** — for artwork that already carries its words, where a name beside it is the name said twice. A category with no picture is drawn with its name either way: it is never an empty square and never left out.
- A category may also carry a **banner**: a wide picture its own page opens with, above the title, drawn in one proportion on every screen so artwork with words in it is never cut differently on a phone. A subcategory with none shows the one of the category it sits in. The search and the list of all products show none.
- A name is unique inside its store, so two products called the same thing cannot both exist and confuse an order.
- A product may be a **kit**: the customer puts it together on its page, picking a set number of items — 2 to 99 — the same product as many times as they like, among the products the kit lets in: **every product of the shop**, **only those of some categories**, or **every one but those of some categories** — a subcategory going with the category it sits under, and a new product following the rule by itself. The kit has **its own price**, whatever is chosen, and **no stock of its own**: what leaves the stock is each product chosen, added to the same product bought loose before it is checked, and a cancelled order gives each back. The kit's page shows what may go in it, a sold-out product marked and not to be chosen, and how many are chosen of the size; it goes into the cart only once whole, with what was chosen, and two kits of one product with other products in them are two lines. A kit is sold while everything chosen for it can be ordered, and leaves the cart when anything chosen left the shop. An order says what went in each kit wherever its lines are read — the panel, the customer's order and receipt, the WhatsApp message. "Buy again" leaves a kit out, saying so: it is put together again on its page. A kit has no variations, a product with variations cannot be one, and no kit goes in a kit.
- There is a separate, product-wide taxonomy of **store categories** ("bakery", "clothing"), which describes stores rather than products.

## Customers

A **customer** is a store's own record of someone who bought from it: a name, a phone number and their addresses, remembered so they do not retype them next time. The phone number identifies them **within one store**: the same person buying from two stores is two customers, and neither store learns about the other.

A customer keeps **several addresses** — home, work — each with a name they give it and who receives there, and **one of them is the default**: where an order goes unless another is chosen at checkout, and the one address the shopkeeper sees on the customer's record.

A customer may be **linked to an account** — the shop's own, opened there — and that link is what lets an address follow them. Opening the account is what makes the person the shop's customer: a lead until they buy. **No shopkeeper ever reads the account's password or sessions**, and no account reaches past its shop, which is what keeps the sentence above literally true: a person who buys from two stores is two customers with two accounts, and neither store can tell.

The link is optional, and a customer without one is not a lesser customer. Shops carried over from the legacy product arrive with no e-mail on file at all.

A signed-in customer keeps **favourites** at the shop: products they liked, one heart per product, with the combination they chose on its page when they chose one. A favourite remembers what it cost the day it was liked, which is how the shop can say it is cheaper now; being on sale or sold out is read from the shop as it is today. Liking it again changes nothing, and choosing another combination is a new like at today's price. A product the shop takes back to draft leaves the list until it returns, and one it deletes leaves for good. A customer who keeps the favourites' notice on is told by e-mail when a favourite gets cheaper while on sale, or comes back in stock — at most once per product in a week, in the shop's name, with the way to switch it off.

**Buying requires no account and no proof of anything.** The shop window, the cart and the checkout open for anyone, so the delivery fee is visible before anything is asked. A visitor buys by giving a **name, a WhatsApp and an e-mail**, then the address and the payment — no password, no code, no account, and the answer is the same whatever the e-mail: having someone's e-mail is no proof of being them, so it is never looked up, only kept on the order to reach whoever bought. The order goes on the shop's record of that phone, or on a new one named as typed, and nothing of that record reaches the visitor — not its name, its addresses, its orders, its CPF or its credit. The visitor's purchase lives for seven days, long enough to pay a Pix, and reads the orders it placed and their payment, and nothing else; the CPF a carrier or an online payment needs is typed every time. Signing in stays an option beside it, never a step of it, and a signed-in customer buys with what their account holds.

## Reviews

A product's **reviews** come from the people the shop delivered it to. A customer rates a product they received — one to five stars, and a few words if they want — once, and may rewrite it later; the review keeps the combination they bought. It is published at once. The shopkeeper cannot edit a review, but may **hide** one from the shop window and publish it again; the customer still sees their own, marked hidden, and rewriting it does not bring it back.

The shop window shows a product's average and how many reviews it has, how many of each rating, and the reviews themselves, newest first. It names each reviewer by their first name and an initial, and as "Cliente" once they no longer have an account.

## Orders

An **order** is the product's core record, and it is a fact about the past.

- It belongs to a store and to a customer, and it carries a **number that is sequential within its store** — something a shopkeeper can say out loud on the phone.
- It holds **lines**: what was bought, how many, and the name and unit price **as they were at the moment of purchase**. A price change or a deleted product never rewrites an order that already happened.
- It records its own totals: the subtotal, the delivery fee, any discount, and the total. Every one of them is a number, not a sentence — with one absence: a delivery's fee may not be agreed yet, and that is never zero, because zero is a free delivery. Until the shop tells the fee, the total is the goods less the discount, and every screen that shows it says the fee is still to come. A pick-up has no fee, and a cancelled order has nothing left to agree.
- It has a **status** the shopkeeper moves it through — received, accepted, being prepared, out for delivery, delivered, or cancelled — and the customer can follow it. A pick-up goes through the same steps under its own names: out for delivery is **ready for pickup**, the step that tells the customer to come, and delivered is **picked up**. A customer with an account hears of it in the order's conversation and by e-mail, as of every other move. A signed-in customer sees their own orders at the shop — the ones they placed and the ones the shop registered for them — with the lines, the totals, where it goes and each status with its time; never the shop's note on it. They may cancel one while it is still **received**; once the shop accepted it, only the shop cancels.
- It says how it is being handed over (delivery or pick-up) and how it will be paid. A delivery goes to one of the customer's addresses and keeps **where it went and who received it, as they were at the moment of purchase**: a customer who moves or changes their name never rewrites an old order, and an order from before the product kept it says it was not recorded rather than borrowing today's address. A delivery with nowhere to go — no street and city — is refused.
- The customer's own note to the shop is the customer's; it is never used to carry the product's own data.

**Money is exact.** Every amount in the product is a whole number of cents. There is one representation, everywhere, and a total shown to a customer matches the total the shopkeeper sees to the cent.

## The order's conversation

An order whose customer has an account has a conversation, born with the order. The customer and the shop write in it while the order is on its way; once it is delivered or cancelled, the conversation closes and stays readable to both.

Every move of the order is told in it as a **notice**: the status it moved to, never a sentence — each side words it for its own reader. A notice is news to the customer, unread until they read it, and never to the shop, whose own doing it is. A move the customer made — placing the order, cancelling it — is not news to them either.

A customer known only by an order, with no account, has no conversation: there is no one to read it.

## Delivery

A store decides whether it delivers, how far, and what it charges. The fee follows the **distance** between the store and the delivery address, within bands the shopkeeper sets, and a free-delivery threshold is one of those decisions. Beyond the store's radius, delivery is refused before the customer fills anything else in — not after.

An estimated arrival is a **window** (from–to), because a single number is a promise the product cannot keep.

A store combines the ways it hands an order over — **pickup** at the shop, its **own delivery**, and **carriers** through its own Melhor Envio account — and each is switched on or off apart. Carriers are quoted with the shop's own account, at its own prices, and the labels are bought from its own wallet: Beelink never resells freight.

The fee is quoted in one place. What the cart shows, what the checkout shows and what the order records are the same quote, read again at the moment the order is placed: a fee that changed since the customer read it refuses the order rather than place it at another price. Where the store set no band, or an address cannot be placed on the map, the fee is **agreed afterwards** — said in words, never as zero, which is a free delivery.

## Promotions and coupons

A store runs **promotions** — a discount over the whole cart, or over named products or categories, for a period the shopkeeper sets. A discount is either a percentage or a fixed amount, and which one it is, is stated rather than inferred. A period has a start and may have no end: it then runs until the shopkeeper pauses it.

A **coupon** is a code a customer types. A code is unique **within its store** whatever the case it is typed in, valid for a period, and may be limited in how many times it can be used in total or by one customer. It gives a discount, as a promotion does, or a free delivery, and may ask for a minimum subtotal. A coupon that has run out, expired or does not apply says so at checkout, before the customer commits.

Only an **identified customer** applies a coupon: whether a code exists is not told to a visitor, who is told the code goes in once they sign in. The answer comes with the cart's totals — taken, or the reason it was not — and an order carries the coupon the customer was shown as applied, and no other. One that stopped holding between that answer and the order refuses the order rather than place it at another price.

A promotion or a coupon may be for a **first purchase** only: a customer with no order at the shop that stands. A cancelled order does not count, so a customer whose only order was cancelled is on a first purchase again. It is checked as the order is written, under the customer's lock, so two orders placed at once never both take it. Nobody can be on a first purchase before they are identified: a visitor's cart announces the offer with what it would take off, and it enters the total once they say who is buying — their phone is what a first purchase is read by — or sign in. A customer who has bought before is told it is not theirs, and why, before they place the order. The shop window is the same for everyone, so a first-purchase promotion never changes a price on it.

**A shop shows of its offers what its shopkeeper chose to show, to whoever it is for.** A coupon's code is private unless the shopkeeper switches on "Mostrar este cupom na loja" — off on every coupon until then, since a code is often one customer's or one partner's. Under the header of the shop's home, its listings and its product pages there is at most one slim strip, in the page and never over it: **anyone with no order that stands** — signed in or not: buying asks no account, and the benefit is theirs either way — is shown the first-order benefit: the shown coupon with its code and the way to the cart that applies it, or the promotion, said to apply by itself; anyone else is shown nothing. **Nothing invites anyone to open an account.** In the **cart**, a signed-in customer sees "Cupons disponíveis": the shown coupons that cart may take, each applied by a press exactly as if typed, one below its minimum saying what is missing instead. Which coupons are listed is decided by the same reading that takes or refuses a code, so none is offered that would be refused. Every number in these sentences is the shop's own record.

**The first-purchase notice is said until it is closed, and then no more; the coupon's reminder lives in the checkout.** Closing the strip — its "×", or using it: following "Usar no carrinho" — is remembered in that browser, for that shop alone, for thirty days, and the strip is not drawn again on the shop's home, listings or product pages: the server reads the memory, so the next page arrives without it. Copying the code closes nothing, and neither does merely seeing the strip — whoever never noticed it does not lose the offer silently. It is the same memory, and the same single number holding nothing of the person, as the pop-up's below: whoever closed the coupon's notice is not told it again, signed in or out; whoever had only ever closed the old invitation to open an account is told the coupon once — the code is news. From then on the place that tells the coupon is the **checkout's coupon**: a signed-in customer with a shown coupon that cart may take, and none applied, sees a highlighted call over the coupon's field — "Você tem 15% de desconto no primeiro pedido com o cupom SEJAMUTANTE" and one button, "Aplicar cupom", which does what typing the code does. It calls to the first-order coupon when there is one, otherwise to the newest the cart may take; never to one below its minimum, which keeps saying what is missing in the list; the others stay under "Cupons disponíveis". It goes when a coupon is applied and cannot be dismissed before: it is in the checkout alone. **The first-order banner's way to the cart — "Usar no carrinho" — brings the coupon applied, for a visitor too, with no sign-in on the way:** a visitor's cart takes a coupon the shop shows in its window, and whether it is their first purchase is read at the order, by the phone they give. Any other code waits until they say who is buying — the cart tells nobody unidentified whether a code exists. A visitor at a shop with a shown first-purchase coupon and none applied reads it — the benefit and the code — in the checkout; a first-purchase promotion is announced by the cart's own totals and is not said twice.

**A shop may tell its first-order benefit with a pop-up, and its shopkeeper sets when.** Switched on in the panel — it is off until the shopkeeper says so — a dialog opens over the shop's home, its listings and its product pages for **anyone with no order that stands**, signed in or not, and never on the cart, the account, the sign-in pages, a landing or the panel. It carries the shop's picture, if it gave one, beside a panel in the shop's own colour, and says the benefit in words that are the product's: "Seu primeiro pedido tem 15% de desconto", the shown coupon's code drawn large, a button that copies it and one that goes to the cart with the coupon applied; a first-purchase promotion has no code, and the dialog says it applies by itself, with one button that closes. **It collects nothing and leads to no sign-up**; with no first-order benefit in force it does not open, and a coupon the shop hides is never announced. For a signed-in customer the benefit is the one their own offers answer — exactly what the strip would tell them. It opens some seconds after the page arrives, or when the visitor is about to leave — the pointer going out through the top of the window; a phone has no such signal, so there it is half the page scrolled or thirty seconds on it. It opens **once**: closed, or with its button pressed, it stays away for thirty days, remembered in that browser for that shop alone and holding nothing of the person — signed in or out, it is one notice; if the shopkeeper changes the pop-up, whoever closed the old one may see the new one once. A browser holding a customer's session the page could not read is told nothing: it may be somebody who has ordered. It waits while the shop's cookie notice is unanswered. What a visitor does with it is counted nowhere.

**With the pop-up on, the dialog is the notice, and after it the shop's pages say nothing — unless the shopkeeper asks for a reminder.** A page never holds both: while the dialog is still due to whoever is looking, the strip is not drawn — the server decides it from the request, so nothing flashes under the dialog. Once the dialog was closed, or its button pressed, nothing of the first purchase is drawn on the home, the listings or the product pages: the cart reminds. "Depois de fechado, manter um lembrete abaixo do cabeçalho" is **off unless switched on**; on, the strip is there from the page after the dialog was closed — it does not arrive at the click, so nothing moves under the pointer — until the strip itself is closed, and then it too is gone for good. A new version of the pop-up — its picture, its words or what it announces changed — may show the dialog, and that reminder, once more. A shop whose pop-up is off has no version to change: there the strip is the notice, and closed is closed for the thirty days.

Promotions never add up. Each line of a cart takes the one promotion worth the most on it — a promotion on a category covers its subcategories — and a fixed amount off the whole cart stands against the lines' own promotions together: the customer gets the larger. The coupon comes after, over what is left of the products, and one order takes one coupon. A percentage is rounded up to the cent, in the customer's favour — a promotion of 10% never reads as 9% — and no total goes below zero.

The discount is computed in one place. What the cart shows, what the checkout shows and what the order records are the same calculation, read at the moment the order is placed. A coupon's limits are checked as the order is written, so two orders at once never both take its last use. A cancelled order gives its coupon's use back.

What came off is said **part by part**, wherever an order or a cart is read: the promotions, the coupon by its code, and what the shopkeeper took off by hand — on the cart, the customer's order and its receipt, their list of orders, the shop's panel and the WhatsApp messages. A free delivery whose fee is not agreed yet is said in words, and to its customer such a total is final: the coupon covers whatever fee is agreed.

While a promotion runs, the shop window shows it: the price a visitor reads is the promotional one, with what it was beside it, and that price times the quantity is what the order takes off. A fixed amount off the whole cart is the exception — it is no product's price, and shows in the cart.

Either can be **paused** and switched back on. Where one stands — scheduled, running, paused, ended, or a coupon used up — is read from the clock and its limits, never set by hand. An order keeps the discount it took as it was, whatever is edited afterwards, and a coupon lists the orders it went into.

## Cashback

A store may give **cashback**: a share of what a customer pays for the products comes back to them as credit, to spend on that store's later orders. The shopkeeper switches it on and sets the rules — the share, how long a credit lasts once given (or that it never expires), the smallest order that earns, and how much of an order credit may pay for. A change to the rules applies to orders placed from then on; credit already given keeps the validity it was given with.

The share is given **one of two ways, and the shopkeeper chooses which**: one percentage for the whole store, or **by product**, where each product carries its own percentage, set where the product is edited. The two never mix. By product, a product with no percentage earns nothing — there is no store-wide share to fall back on — and the list of products says each one's share and offers to add one where there is none. A product keeps its percentage while the store is on one share for all, and it is read again the day the store goes back. The smallest order that earns, the validity and how much credit may pay for stay the store's either way.

**Credit is the store's, not money.** It belongs to the customer's record at that store, is spent only there, and is never withdrawn, transferred or seen by another store. A customer with no account earns and spends it too, through the record the shop keeps of them.

An order **earns** on the products, after the promotions, the coupon and any credit spent on it — never on the delivery. By product, each line earns at its product's percentage; a coupon, a discount typed by hand and credit spent belong to the whole order, so they come off every line in proportion to what it costs, and the order records the average it earned at. The credit is **pending** while the order is open and becomes **usable once the order is delivered**, the same proof of a sale reviews rest on, since most payments are settled out of the product's sight; its validity counts from the delivery. An order that is cancelled, or that leaves *delivered*, takes back what it earned — and when the customer already spent that credit, the balance **stops at zero**: it never goes negative, and the shopkeeper is shown the difference.

The shop window says what comes back while the cashback is on — on a product, at that product's own percentage where the store gives by product (and nothing on a product with none), and in the cart with what the order would earn or what is missing to reach the minimum — and each order says what it earned and where that credit stands. A customer who deletes their account loses their credit: the statement says so, and the shop's record of them may stay for its books. Credit is **spent** at checkout, after the promotions and the coupon, on the products only — never the delivery — and at most the share of them the shopkeeper allows; the soonest-to-expire credit goes first. The customer is told what they have and the most the cart takes before they confirm, and an order asking for more than they can spend at that moment is refused rather than placed at another price. Credit already given can be spent even after the shopkeeper switches the cashback off. Credit spent on an order that is then cancelled comes back with the validity it had — and at least seven more days, so it can still be used. Credit **expires** by itself once its validity is over, leaving the balance with a line on the statement; a week before, the customer is told by e-mail, unless they turned that notice off — it starts on, since it is about credit they already hold, not an offer.

Each customer's credit is a **statement** that only grows: what was earned, spent, taken back, expired, and what the shopkeeper adjusted by hand, with their reason. A line is never edited; a mistake is answered by another line. The balance is what the statement adds up to, and every change to it happens under the customer's lock, so two orders at once never spend the same credit. The shopkeeper sees what the store owes in credit, and how much of it expires soon. The customer reads their own in their account at the shop — the balance, what is pending, each credit with its validity and the statement — without the shopkeeper's reason for an adjustment, which was written for the shop's books. A sale the shopkeeper registers in the panel can spend the chosen customer's credit too, by the same rule as the checkout.

## Affiliates

A store may have **affiliates**: people it invites to sell through a link of its own, who earn a **commission** on what they sell. The customer pays the store's own prices; the affiliate's share is worked out by bee-link and paid by the shopkeeper, outside it.

- **The programme is the store's**, and starts switched off. The shopkeeper sets whether it is on; how many days a visit through a link credits the orders that follow it (1 to 90, 30 to start with); how many days after a payout period closes its commissions fall due (0 to 60, 10 to start with); and the programme's own terms, which an affiliate accepts on joining. A different text of the terms is a new version, and each affiliate keeps the one they accepted.
- **Commission comes from tables.** Every store has a default table, born with the programme at 10% and never deleted; a special condition is another table, up to fifty. A table has one rate for every product and, where the shopkeeper sets one, a product's own rate in its place — 0% means the product pays nothing. An item's rate is its product's on the affiliate's table, or else the table's, and nothing else. A product's form and the list of products edit the default table. A table with affiliates on it is not deleted.
- **An affiliate is invited, and chooses their own name.** The shopkeeper gives a name, an e-mail and a table, and the invite goes to that e-mail: valid seven days and once, sent again at most once a minute, each new one ending the last link. The person invited reads the store, their rates and the terms. They sign up with that e-mail, which the invite proves, or sign in to the bee-link account they have — another account is refused, saying whom the invite is for. They choose their `/name`, which can never be a word the store's addresses use, a category of the store's (now or before a rename) or another affiliate's; a category can no longer take an affiliate's name either. They may give a Pix key, and accept the terms they read — changed meanwhile, they read them again. An invite cancelled before it is accepted leaves nothing behind.
- **An affiliate's account is bee-link's**, at the shopkeeper's door: one account sells for any number of stores, and may own stores too. At a store, an affiliate is invited, active, paused — crediting nothing new while what was credited runs its course — or ended, for good. An ended affiliate's history stays, and the same e-mail may be invited again into a new standing. A name stays the affiliate's at that store for good, ended or not, so a printed link never credits someone else.
- **The link** is `/<store>/<name>` — `minhaloja.com.br/<name>` on a store's own domain — or any page of the store with `?afiliado=<name>`. It opens the store with a card under the header saying whose recommendation the visitor came by, with the affiliate's photo or initials and their phrase. The rest of the visit carries a thin strip the visitor may close, and the cart and the checkout always say "Indicação de <name> — ele verá seu nome neste pedido". Prices and the catalogue never change. A paused or ended affiliate's link, or one at a store whose programme is off, opens the store as it is — never a 404 — and credits nobody.
- **The visit is kept in the visitor's browser** as the affiliate and the instant, nothing of the person, for the programme's days, and **the last link wins**. It waits for no consent: it is the store's agreement with its affiliate, not marketing. Visits are counted as the funnel counts — a number per affiliate per day, no visitor, never a browser with the panel open, kept thirteen months.
- **Nothing of it is live before the privacy policy that tells of it.** Until a deployment is switched on (`AFFILIATE_LINKS_ENABLED`), no link credits, no cookie is written and no order can be given an affiliate; the switch follows the policy's publication.
- **An order is credited when it is placed**, to the affiliate of the visit, if at that moment the affiliate is active, the programme on and the visit within its days. Otherwise it goes in credited to nobody, without a word to the customer. The shopkeeper may credit a sale they register in the panel, and may take an order's affiliate away or change it while its commission is pending, with a reason kept in the order's history; once the commission is available, a correction is an adjustment.
- **The commission is worked out as cashback by product is**: on what the customer paid for the products, after the promotions, with a coupon, a discount typed by hand and credit spent shared among the lines in proportion to their price — never the delivery — at each line's rate, with the table's name and every rate photographed on the order. A purchase by the affiliate themselves — the customer's e-mail or phone is theirs — is credited at zero, said so, and told to nobody.
- **It is paid by a calendar.** A month has three periods — the 1st to the 10th, the 11th to the 20th, the 21st to its last day — and a commission falls in the period of the day its order was **delivered**, due on that period's last day plus the programme's delay: at ten days, the 20th, the 30th, and the 10th of the next month. Pending until then, it becomes available on its due day by itself. An order cancelled before that undoes it for good; one that leaves *delivered* sends it back to waiting, and a new delivery gives a new due day; one cancelled after it was available is taken back by a negative line, and the balance may go below zero, to be settled from what comes next. A partial refund changes nothing by itself, and the order tells the shopkeeper so.
- **The affiliate is told by e-mail** of each sale credited to them — with the customer's name, the items and the commission expected — of each such order cancelled, and of each payout registered.
- **Each affiliate's money is a statement that only grows**: released, taken back, adjusted by the shopkeeper with a reason both of them read, paid out. A line is never edited. The shopkeeper sees what is owed grouped by due day, what is overdue and what waits for a delivery, and **registers a payout** made outside bee-link: an amount up to the available balance, the day, how (Pix, transfer or cash) and a note.
- **The affiliate's area is `/afiliado`**: the stores they sell for and, at each, their links — every active product's too, with its rate — the last thirty days' visits, orders, sales and commission, their orders, the statement, the payouts, what is available now and the next due day, and their own data: the Pix key, the phone, a photo and a phrase, the last two shown on the store's window. They read the name of each customer who bought through them, and that order; never the customer's e-mail, phone, CPF or address. The shopkeeper may take an affiliate's photo or phrase off. An affiliate's area answers even at a store locked for want of payment: what is owed to them does not vanish.

## Checkout and the handoff to WhatsApp

**The cart and the checkout are two pages.** The cart is what was chosen — each line with its quantity, what it comes to as an estimate, how it splits on a card and that the delivery is priced at checkout — with one way on, "Finalizar compra", and under it **"Você também pode gostar"**: more of the shop, never what is in the cart already or sold out, each with "Adicionar ao carrinho", so a product goes into the cart from the cart and the total follows at once. The checkout is a page of its own and nothing else: the shop's logo, back to the shop, and the bag, back to the cart — no search, no menu, no banners, no pop-ups — then the summary of the order, closed to one line on a phone, the coupon behind "Inserir cupom de desconto", and the three steps. A checkout with nothing in the cart to order is the cart.

Checkout collects who the customer is, where the order goes, how it will be paid, and any coupon — then places the order and hands the conversation to **WhatsApp** — or, for an order paid online, takes the customer on to its payment. A shop that prepares its orders from WhatsApp may ask an order paid online to be sent there too: once the payment is confirmed, the screen that says so offers the shop's WhatsApp with the message ready — the order's number, its lines, where it goes and that it is already paid — and waits for that press rather than moving on. It is off until the shop switches it on, and nothing is opened before the money arrived: sent before, it could take the customer away from a Pix not yet paid. The order exists in the product the moment it is placed; the WhatsApp message is how the shopkeeper and the customer keep talking, which is how this trade already works in Brazil. It names the order by its number, and its lines, its discounts and its total are the ones the product recorded, not the ones the page computed.

A sale the shopkeeper registers in the panel is priced the same way before it is saved: the summary they confirm is the total the product will record, a promotion running on the day of the sale included.

An order placed at checkout starts **received**: the shopkeeper accepts it, or cancels it. A shop with no WhatsApp still takes orders — the customer is told the shop will confirm.

**The product never receives or holds money.** An order is paid in one of two ways, and the order says which.

*Settled with the shop*, as it always was: the payment method is a label saying how the two of them agreed — on delivery, at pickup, by a transfer between them — and nothing is charged by anyone here.

*Paid online*, where the shopkeeper connected their own **Asaas** account and chose to take it: Pix, or a credit card in interest-free instalments up to the number the shop offers. The charge is created **in the shop's own Asaas account** and the money goes straight there; bee-link is never in its path, takes no commission, and keeps no card — a card is typed on Asaas's own page, never in the shop window. A shopkeeper may keep both ways side by side, or turn paying on delivery off.

Paying online asks the customer's CPF, once. The customer pays without leaving the shop: a Pix shows its QR code and its copy-and-paste code right after the order is placed, and a card opens Asaas's page beside it. An order placed and not yet paid **exists** — the shop sees it, and the customer comes back to pay from the order, with a new Pix when the first one expired. Only a closed total is charged: an order whose delivery fee is still to be agreed is paid once the shop tells the fee. An order with nothing left to pay — a coupon or credit covered it — is charged nothing and settled with the shop.

**An order is paid when Asaas says so to the product**, never because a page was reached or a browser said it. Until then it reads *awaiting payment*, and it turns to *payment approved* by itself. A paid order is not cancelled without its money being given back: its customer cannot cancel it and asks the shop, and the shop cancels it with the refund in the same act — the order is cancelled only once Asaas took the refund.

**The shopkeeper gives money back from the order**, all of it or a part, saying why. The refund is asked of the shop's own Asaas account; the customer sees it on the order — how much, and whether it went back or is on its way, which on a card takes days — reads it in the order's conversation and is e-mailed once. The reason stays with the shop. Money that arrived without the order asking for it — paid after it was cancelled, or paid twice — is given back the same way, and stops being flagged once it is.

A shop with no Asaas account connected, or whose connection needs mending, sells exactly as before: every order is settled with the shop.

## The shop's ad pixel

A store may connect **its own Meta Pixel**, by its ID alone, so that Meta can measure and aim the ads the shopkeeper buys there. The pixel, the ads and the reports are the shopkeeper's, in their own Meta account: bee-link advertises with none of it and shows no result of it.

- **A visitor is asked first, by each shop.** A shop with a pixel asks whoever opens it to accept or refuse, and the two answers weigh the same. Until a yes, nothing of Meta's is loaded and nothing is sent. Every shop lives on one domain, so the answer is the shop's: a yes given at one is not a yes at another.
- **After a yes, the shop tells Meta the visitor's path through it**: each page opened, a product seen, a search made, a product liked, a product put in the cart, the checkout reached and a way of paying picked — with the product and its value in reais where there is one, and never which way of paying it was. What the visitor did before the yes is not told afterwards.
- **A purchase is told with the order's total and what was bought, and counted once.** At a shop that settles payment with its customer, the order placed is the purchase; at a shop that charges on the site, the payment confirmed is — an order never paid is none. Only an order the customer placed from the cart counts, never one the shopkeeper registered. The buyer's own browser tells it while they are looking at the order, in the day after it counted, and a yes given there afterwards tells it then. An order cancelled or refunded later is not taken back from Meta.
- **A shop that gives its pixel's access token has the purchase told from bee-link's server too**, so it reaches Meta when the buyer closed the page or blocks Meta's script. It is the same purchase under the same identifier, which is what lets Meta count the two as one. The server tells it only for a buyer whose yes stood when the order was placed — the yes is kept with the order — and then it carries the buyer's e-mail and phone turned into a code (SHA-256) that Meta can match and nobody can read back, with what the order kept of their browser. A buyer who deleted their account before it went is not told of. A send that fails never touches the order: it is tried again for up to seven days, and a token Meta refuses stops the telling, and says so in the panel, until the shopkeeper gives another.
- **An order remembers which campaign brought its buyer.** A visitor who lands on a shop by a link with campaign labels (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) has them kept for that shop alone, for thirty days — the last campaign to bring them is the one kept, and a visit with none changes nothing. The order they place records it, and the shopkeeper reads it on the order's page: "facebook / cpc · campanha X", or a direct visit. This is the shop's own account of its sales, kept with or without a pixel and whatever the visitor answered about cookies. Meta's own identifier of an ad's click is another matter: it is kept only on a yes, never shown, and the order's page says only that an ad of Meta's brought the buyer. A sale the shopkeeper registers by hand has no origin.
- **The shopkeeper reads what each campaign sold, in the panel.** "Vendas por origem" lists a period's sales by the campaign that brought each buyer, with the orders, the amount and its share: a campaign's labels, a direct visit, an ad's click with no labels, and — a line of their own — the sales the shopkeeper registered in the panel, so the lines add up to what the shop sold. A sale is an order not cancelled, counted on the day it was placed for its total; one charged on the site counts once it is paid, as a purchase told to Meta does. An order from before origins were kept reads as direct. It works with or without a pixel, reads only what bee-link stored, and asks Meta nothing: what the ads cost, and their return, stay at Meta.
- **Every event goes to that shop's pixel and to no other**, whatever shops the visitor opened before it.
- **A yes can be taken back at any time**, from the shop's footer, and the shop stops telling Meta at once. Meta's own identifiers in the browser belong to the whole domain and stay until they expire: another shop's yes may rest on them.
- Nothing is told from the panel, from a page being designed, or from a page opened by a single-use link — confirming an e-mail, setting a password.
- From the browser, the shop sends no name, e-mail or phone with an event: what reaches Meta beside it is what any request carries — the address it came from and the browser — and Meta's own identifier. From the server, a purchase carries the e-mail and the phone as codes and never the name, the address, the CPF or the address of the buyer's network. What the shopkeeper switches on in their own Meta account is theirs to answer for.

## The shop's funnel

Every shop that sells reads, in its panel, **where its customers give up**: "Funil da loja" shows a period's visits, products seen, additions to the cart, checkouts begun and purchases, each step with how many that is for every hundred of the step before and how many were lost on the way. It needs no pixel and no ad campaign.

- **It counts events, never people — and that is why it asks nobody's consent.** What bee-link keeps of a visit is a number: per shop, per day, per step. There is no row for a visitor or a session, no cookie written or read to count, no identifier, no address, no browser. A visitor who opens five products counts five; the rates are "N for every hundred", not the share of visitors who bought. Because nothing follows anyone, it is counted at every shop and for every visitor, whatever they answered about cookies — the consent strip is about Meta, and this tells Meta nothing.
- **The steps are the moments the shop's pixel is told of**: a page opened, once per page; a product's page opened; a product put in the cart, from any button; the cart reached with something that can be ordered. Nothing is counted in the panel, in a page being designed, on a page opened by a single-use link, or in a browser where a panel is signed in — a shopkeeper looking at a shop is not a visit.
- **The purchase is not a count: it is the shop's own orders.** The last step is the sales "Vendas por origem" reads, by the same rule, that customers placed on the shop window. A sale the shopkeeper registered in the panel never walked the funnel and is said apart. Purchases are counted from the first day the shop's visits were, so no rate is drawn over days nothing was counted on.
- **A step can be larger than the one before**, since a product goes into the cart from a card without its page being opened; the page says so rather than hide it.
- **A day's numbers are kept for thirteen months and then deleted** — long enough to set a month beside the same month a year before. Days from before the funnel existed read as zero.
- A site that sells nothing has no funnel.

## bee-link's own site, measured

bee-link measures **its own site** with Google Analytics — the landing page, and the two screens a shopkeeper signs in and signs up at — to know how many people arrive and how many create an account. The measurement is bee-link's, in its own Google Analytics account: no shop reads it, and it measures no shop.

- **A visitor is asked first, on the landing page**, to accept or refuse, and the two answers weigh the same. Until a yes, nothing of Google's is loaded and nothing is sent; after a no, the same. The sign-in and the sign-up ask nothing: they go by the answer given on the landing page, and whoever reaches them without having answered is not measured.
- **The answer is the site's, never a shop's.** A yes to bee-link is not a yes to any shop's cookie notice, and a shop's is not a yes to bee-link.
- **After a yes, three things are told**: each of those three pages opened, an account created — that one was, and nothing of who created it — and a courier's form taken, with nothing of what was typed in it. A page is told by its address without what the address carries: only a campaign's labels (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`, `utm_term`) stay. No name, e-mail, phone or CPF is sent. Where the visitor came from is told without its parameters, and a page of bee-link itself only as "bee-link" — never which shop, which order, or which link with a token.
- **It is not for advertising.** Nothing is linked to an ad profile and no ad storage is asked for. That stops being true the day bee-link's Google Analytics account is linked to an advertising one — which is a decision about the privacy policy before it is one about the account.
- **Never at a shop, in the panel, or on a page opened by a single-use link** — confirming an e-mail, setting a password. A visitor who moves from a measured page to any other is told of no further.
- **A yes can be taken back at any time**, from the landing page's footer: bee-link stops telling Google at once and takes Google Analytics' identifiers out of the browser.
- **A deployment measures only where it was told what to measure with.** Without that — homologation, a developer's machine — nobody is asked and nothing is loaded.

## People who ask to deliver

bee-link keeps a **list of people who asked to deliver** for its shops. It is a list of interest and nothing else: there is no courier's account, no courier's app and no delivery handed to anyone yet.

- **The landing page asks** for a full name, a CPF, an e-mail, a WhatsApp number and how the person delivers, and for the terms of use and the privacy policy to be accepted — both read over the form, without leaving it. That is the first step of a sign-up; the second, the documents, is said on the page and does not exist yet.
- **One city is open today: Fortaleza.** A city is opened by bee-link; it is not written in by whoever applies, and while there is one the page does not ask: every entry made there is for it.
- **A person is on the list once.** A CPF, an e-mail or a WhatsApp number already there is not taken, and what was kept is left as it was, so nobody who knows a CPF can write over the person it belongs to. For a CPF the page says the person is signed up already, where the confirmation would stand, and counts nobody as new; an e-mail or a WhatsApp number is said on its own field, since it may have been mistyped. The price is that whoever types one of the three learns whether it is on the list.
- **The entry keeps which version of the terms was accepted, and when.**
- **Whoever is taken onto the list is told so by e-mail, once.** The message says bee-link has the application, for which city, and that the guidance to go on with it comes later to that same address; it greets by the first name, carries no link and asks for nothing. An address is written to once, whoever was on the list before this began included. It goes to the address as it was typed, which nobody confirmed — so a person who never applied may receive it, once.
- **Beyond that message, the product writes to nobody.** The guidance itself is not sent by bee-link, and nobody is approved or refused by it. Whoever answers these people does it by hand, from the list.
- **Nothing deletes or corrects an entry by itself.** Sending the form again changes nothing: a person asks through the channel the privacy policy gives, and the entry is corrected or taken out by hand.

## What the product does not do

- It does not receive, hold or move money: an online payment happens in the shopkeeper's own Asaas account, and what is settled with the shop is between the two of them.
- It does not pay affiliates: it works out what a store owes each of them and records what the shopkeeper says was paid, outside bee-link. No payment is split for one.
- It does not send WhatsApp messages on the shopkeeper's behalf — it opens the conversation.
- It has no cross-store identity: a customer's account belongs to the shop they signed up at.
- It is not a marketplace: there is no page that lists every store, and no store discovers another's customers.
